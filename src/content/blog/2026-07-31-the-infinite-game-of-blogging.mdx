What do you blog for?

For years, I’ve been telling myself that I blog to bring in customers for my course business — Content Marketing, as they say. But that’s not true. Content Marketing was, really, just a side effect. When I focused on it, I began to lose my way.

I forgot about the real reason I started this blog, since it started in a moment of unconsciousness, a spark, so to speak. That spark prompted the creation of this place you see here today.

Even the reason I gave when I [first started](/blog/why-i-started-learning-about-web-design-and-how-you-can-do-it-too/) is just a smidgen of the truth — perhaps way less than "Content Marketing" — because I had been trapped in a worldview where I _had_ to help people (and that helping people is a good thing).

**Both views eventually caused the burnout.** The inconsistent posts. The stopping. And even the difficulty in staying consistent after I restarted.

## On Infinite Games

I discovered the concept of [infinite games](https://seths.blog/2014/05/the-short-game-the-long-game-and-the-infinite-game/) many years ago, but I couldn’t comprehend what it meant.

The gist is: Finite games are those that have an end — either you win or lose. **Infinite games are games you play to continue playing** — you win by continuing to play.

Yesterday, I realized that blogging is an infinite game.

Then, two questions came up.

- Do I wanna keep playing this game? Yeah.
- What do I need to keep playing this game?

Then most of what held up this blog for so long came crumbling down — the need to deliver value, the need to teach, wanting to show "the messy middle", etc.

I realized this.

**What one needs to keep playing an infinite game** — is for every action one takes, every move one makes, to help one continue to play it — better.

This realization eventually led to a re-definition of what this blog is for.

## The blog exists for another reason now

**The purpose of this blog** is to help myself grow to become the person I wanna be. While doing so, attract people who love the direction I'm going in, so we can chat, interact, and make stuff happen, together or otherwise.

There are a few points I look for when determining whether each content piece is publishable. They are:

1. I'm writing what I wanna write about
2. My voice is not contorted by the intentions of selling, getting attention, seeking assurance, being accepted, or other forms.
3. Can I proudly stamp me, the current me, onto this piece?
4. Do I grow a little by publishing this?

If my answer is yes to all of these four questions, then I know a piece is good enough to publish.

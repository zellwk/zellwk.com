If you are stuck in Tutorial Hell, you’re not alone.

I’m not sure if it gives you comfort knowing you’re not alone, but if it does, I want you to know that many other people get stuck in Tutorial Hell too.

There is a way out of Tutorial Hell — and I’m going to show you how.

But first, you must understand why we all get trapped in it in the first place.

(Yes, even I get into Tutorial Hell when I learn new technologies).

## Why We’re All Trapped

We’re trapped because most tutorials on the Internet have the same problems:

1. They only explain surface level concepts
2. The regurgitate off other tutorials they found online

If you don’t believe me, just try Googling any JavaScript concept and look at the top 10 articles. You’ll no doubt see similar same arguments and code examples being repeated over and over again.

**This creates a problem for all of us on two levels**:

1. We need to understand concepts on a deeper level before we can use them.
2. But we cannot find information to deepen our understanding.

Still, we find ourselves Googling because we unconsciously believe that we can find everything on the Internet. Unfortunately, this believe is simply not true.

This is likely the main reason why you ended up in Tutorial Hell, and why you remain stuck in it.

Which means, if you want to get out of Tutorial Hell, you have to change your learning behaviour.

## Ways Out of Tutorial Hell

We have to deepen our understanding to get out of Tutorial Hell.

So there are only two ways out:

1. We either [figure it out ourselves](https://zellwk.com/blog/figure-it-out/).
2. Or we learn from someone who is willing to explain beyond the surface level

**The first way is a tough, long, and lonely road with many winding turns**. If you go on this path, you will also have to fight imposter syndrome as you battle your understanding against what others are saying. (They’re not always right).

This route takes effort, dedication, discipline, and time — usually lots and lots of time.

**The second way is much easier.**

We simply piggyback on people who are willing to teach us. We absorb their knowledge and be able to use it with much lesser effort and time.

The main challenge here is finding people who are willing to teach beyond the common surface understanding of things. Another problem is these people often charge a premium for their knowledge and the work they do — which is hard to obtain in the first place.

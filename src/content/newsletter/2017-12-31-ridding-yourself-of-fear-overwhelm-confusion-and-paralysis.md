**This email is important.** **If you only take one thing away from me in 2017 and 2018 combined, let it be this**. I'm going to share with you how to rid yourself of your fear, confusion, overwhelm and paralysis.

You’ll learn to do the work you need to do, and learn skills you want to learn, regardless of whether you believe you can do it.

Read it carefully.

**It’s okay to feel afraid, overwhelmed, confused, or paralysed.**

These feelings are natural. They’re there to protect you from anything dangerous, like getting eaten by a tiger. When you’re afraid, you’ll run away; you’ll hide.

Yes, it sounds silly to compare the fear/overwhelm/confusion/paralysis of learning things with getting eaten by a tiger, but these feelings are generated by our survival mechanism. They feel real.

The bad news is, there’s no way to avoid these feelings. The good news is, you can overcome them.

**Overcoming the feelings of fear, overwhelm, confusion, and paralysis**

There are two ways to overcome the feelings of fear, overwhelm, confusion and paralysis.

The **first way** is to put yourself in a situation where you don’t have the luxury to worry about failure. You either do, or die.

I placed myself in this situation in the encounter with my client. I had to create the prototype in four days, or I can forget about being hired as a freelance frontend developer forever (at least that’s what’s in my mind).

This option works, but is obviously risky. I don’t recommend it. But since I’m crazy, I often put myself in situations like this 😅.

The **second way** is to expect these feelings to show up (They will). Then, push on and continue learning even when you feel afraid, overwhelmed, confused or paralysed.

To push on, you need to have a goal. Your goal needs to be something concrete, like “build myself an accordion with vanilla JavaScript”, so you can see and touch it.

This goal must be something slightly out of reach (so you stay interested to work towards it). It mustn’t be so far out of reach that you give up without trying. It should also not be something you know you can do, because… what’s the point?

Then ask yourself this question:

“_What’s the smallest possible step you can take to reach your goal?_”

Then do it. If you don’t know anything about it, search for it, then experiment. You’ll come up with multiple wrong answers before you get the right one, and once you get the right one, you’ll internalize it.

Don’t worry about anything else; not about getting hired, not about learning fast or slow, not about whether you’re right or wrong, not about the quality of your code, not about what others would say about you.

**What if you don’t have the time?**

This is what most people struggle with. Time.

If you want to learn something, you need to carve out time. Everyone in the world works with 24 hours a day. Nobody has 26 hours, so nobody has extra time.

That means you need to prioritize.

You need to say no to something.

When I taught myself JavaScript, I said no to TV, nights out with friends, games and hobbies.

Even when I write this email to you, I say no to TV and other goodies too. (So, don’t hit me up on a conversation with blockbuster TV series. I’ll stare at you and give you a blank face, because I know nothing about them).

If you say yes to learning \[insert your skill\], what are you saying no to?

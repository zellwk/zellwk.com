title: "\"What if I don't want a job?\""
---

If you don’t want a job, don’t get a job.

Life is too short to be doing something you don’t want to do.

Doesn’t this sound ironic when I’m talking about finding a job?

**That’s because jobs are only stepping stones to something much larger**. After all, you’re transitioning into web development because of one of these two reasons:

- You want a better life
- You want more control

Maybe both. **You want control over your life and you want to make it better.**

So **you are going to be unsatisfied with a job**. You want something more. Eventually.

If that’s you, good! Let me know because I’m like this too.

When I started my coding career, I went straight to freelance. The only “jobs” I had were all freelance jobs.

I could never get a “real” job because I didn’t allow myself to.

For a good reason.

Recently, I have the opportunity to coach someone who got into an intermediate level web developer job. This guy completed half of my Learn JavaScript course, picked up Vue in 1.5 days, and passed the technical interview with relative ease.

A few months into the job, he asked me whether he was cut out to learn JavaScript. This question felt weird since he was a positive guy and he enjoyed the course before.

So why?

I hopped onto call with him and we discovered the real reason.

This is a person who never wanted to get a job. But he got a job because his clients weren’t able to pay during Covid. He had to get a job to survive.

Now that he got one, he began to dread everything coding. But before the job, he was happy learning to code.

Why?

Because he didn’t honour what he really wants — freedom. He wanted to have the ability to control his time. He didn’t want to be limited to a 9-5 job. He also wanted to build his own future, not someone else’s.

Essentially, he was driving his life with both the brakes and accelerator pedal on at the same time. It was a big waste of energy.

A couple of minutes into the call, he realized the problem and he began brightening up. He’s now making a transition from a full-time developer back into a freelance role.

He’s happier now. He’s learning faster. He’s moving faster.

I’m sharing this story with you because it’s going to help you if you don’t want a job. **You reason you might not have a job despite your best attempts, is because you actually didn’t want one.**

Which is great!

**You don’t need a job to kickstart your development career**. Everyone says you should get a job. But that’s not true.

**What you really need is a way to earn money while getting paid to code** — and a job is simply a stepping stone to the ultimate goal of coding for a living.

Better yet — code something, sell it, and you don’t even need to be there to earn a living. So you have your time back and you can spend it with your loved ones.

The problem with holding a job that’s not related to coding, is you have to be a superman or a superwoman — working 2 jobs (your normal day job and a student’s job) while taking care of your children (if you have any). That’s impossible unless you are made of iron. And you’re not. You’re human just like the rest of us.

So if you want to code for a living, you have to begin coding for a living. Finding a job is simply the first step.

If you don’t want a job, then finding freelancing work is your first step. This is doable too as demonstrated by yours truly.

There are too many materials out there stating that a frontend developer role needs to be able to hit an extremely high level of skill.

This is false information. Bloated information. Bloated because people think of frontend developers as a typical kind of person who has those skills. They want those skills.

But they don’t really want the person. What they really want is to have their problems solved.

So **what you really need is an ability to spot problems and solve problems. You also need to ability to connect with people**, so you can spot their problems and solve their problems.

Ultimately, this is what everyone is doing when they get hired — be it for a job, for freelance, or even if they produce a service as a business. This is what a course like Learn JavaScript does too.

You need to spot problems and solve problems.

You need to connect with people so you can spot their problems and solve their problems.

That’s it.

If you have this skill, you’ll go anywhere. You will be able to code for a living. You can find a decent-paying job anywhere. You can freelance for a living too.

What I’m teaching in the course is applicable to both paths — because we’re talking about the principles that work.

It’s what happens before your resume or portfolio begins to work It is what gets you a back door into the job (if you want a job). It is what gets you a freelance client (if you want to freelance).

Here’s one simple framework to stand out from your peers at any level.

It's one of the simplest and most powerful frameworks you can use to get yourself hired anywhere — be it as an employee or a freelancer.

**Go beyond what your peers offer.**

Originally, I was thinking of sharing this framework (and many powerful ones) in the workshop. But I decided to share some of them with you now. What I’m going to do next is simply increase the level of quality you’re going to get in the [workshop](https://store.zellwk.com/find-your-first-dev-job/). That makes me excited.

Ok. With that said, let me put this framework into context for you.

On a very simple level, we can categorize all jobs into three levels:

- Junior
- Intermediate
- Senior

People who want to be hired as junior developers all think the same way. If you want to stand out, you have to go beyond what junior developers think and offer.

Here’s an example.

For most junior developers, their primary focus is to learn. When they apply for jobs, they want to have a chance to develop their skills. If you think this way, then you’re going to be a commodity like the rest.

The only way to stand out is to position yourself from the perspective of an intermediate developer. This makes you an instant hire.

Why?

Companies don’t want to hire you to train you. Companies want to hire you to solve their problems. Intermediate developers know how to solve problems and they don’t need as much hand-holding. They’re also better employees who don’t give problems because they can (at the very least) contribute to the company and not take away from the company.

I expand this concept a bit more in [this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lr312DGmPWQ). It’s free. Go check it out.

We can also expand the same concept to intermediate developers.

Most intermediate developers think the same way. They simply want to code and make stuff. If you want to code and make stuff, you’re the same as other intermediate developers, which means you don’t stand out.

The straightforward answer is to think like a senior developer. Seniors are willing to teach and share and prioritize the team’s results over their individual ones. Once you switch to that perspective, you begin to do senior-related things and you will definitely get hired as an intermediate developer. Some may even hire you as a senior.

In a nutshell

- To get hired as a junior, think like an intermediate
- To get hired as an intermediate, think like a senior
- To get hired as a senior… you get the drift.

**Don’t discount this subtle shift in perspective.**

This may seem like a subtle shift. But this shift changes the way you play the game. It puts you way ahead of the rest of your peers.

Learn to observe the subtlety. (When you get to a higher level, you’ll notice that all breakthroughs are caused by subtle shifts in mindset, orientation, and perspective).

Btw, You be might be wondering what this “automate your workflow” fuss is all about. If you do, let me explain.

Automate Your Workflow is my latest book. It aims to teach you how to automate your frontend development workflow using a tool called Gulp.

It was tough to come up with the contents of this book. I didn’t want it to turn out like other Gulp books, where they focus solely on teaching you about the tool.

What I wanted to achieve was to give you a guide that lets you learn to setup your workflow from scratch. By the time you finish reading this book, you’d already have a workflow, plus you’ll confident enough to improve on it.

This is an ambitious goal, which means the book itself is equally ambitious.

There are eight sections in total.

The first section walks you through the basics of optimization, teaching you things like how to begin the journey, giving you a overview of the topic, how to set up your project etc.

In the second to seventh sections, we dive deep into the six parts of an automated development workflow to figure out how to automate each of them.

Finally, we learn how to organize the workflow, and how to find good plugins.

There’s a lot more where that came from, so check out the [table of contents](http://automateyourworkflow.com/#what-s-in-the-book-) to find out more.

In the nutshell, this book is for you if:

- You want to learn to use better processes to speed up your workflow
- Your want to work less, get more done and become more effective
- You want to have minions doing repetitive tasks so you don’t have to do them anymore
- You want to have more time to spend on stuff that matters.

The best part is you don’t have to be an experience developer in order to get started with automation.

**This book is written specifically for people want to have a better workflow, but are not sure how to start and where to start.**

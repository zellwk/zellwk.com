I want to thank everyone who responded to the [survey](https://ckarchive.com/b/38uphkhenlpl) regarding the membership. You've helped me look deeper into this offering to get more clarity about what this thing really is for.

Many people expressed concerns about the vagueness of this membership project and are hesitant about putting money behind it. I totally understand.

Today, I want to have a deeper conversation about what this is for and why it's important.

If you don't want any further discussion about this membership project, [click here](https://zellwk.com/gotcha).

Let's start by killing the Pink Elephant in the room.

## Is this about the money?

No.

I have a plan to monetize the Web Dev / Tech side of the blog. (I'm going to use Web Dev and Tech interchangeably here). The idea is to create mini-courses, article series, cheatsheets, etc, and place them behind a paywall. These materials are small enough for you to quickly refer to, so they're super valuable. They're also easier to produce compared to large courses like Learn JavaScript.

I don't want to dive deep into the tech side today. You'll hear about this strategy from me in the coming months.

The membership project we're talking today is a separate membership group. A separate paywall, so to speak. **In this project, we're talking about non-tech things.**

If you're the kind of person who only wants to hear from me regarding the Tech side of things, you can [click here](https://zellwk.com/gotcha) and skip this membership discussion.

## What's the purpose of this membership project?

This membership project helps me create a space where I can talk about non-tech stuff. It gives me a chance to understand myself more, and a chance to express myself in ways that I couldn't before.

But why do I want to talk about non-tech stuff?

My blog is my home on the internet. I want to talk about things I'm interested in on this blog. In the beginning, I'm interested in exploring tech stuff, so I wrote tech articles.

These tech articles eventually gained a following and I managed to turn it into a full-time business through Learn JavaScript.

Unfortunately, the business I created consumed me. Since my livelihood depends on what I put onto the blog, I became scared to share anything that may derail the business. I stuck strictly to tech articles (although some non-tech articles still made their way into my calendar, and I'll elaborate why later).

My interests have grown beyond tech, and I've been unhappy because I haven't been able to express things I want to write about. My online home was taken over by the my business, and I was no longer who I am.

The suppression eventually [caused the depression I spoke about a couple of weeks ago](https://ckarchive.com/b/k0umh6hkge2x).

### Why don't I simply just write about stuff I enjoy?

In theory, I can write anything I want on my blog. Unfortunately, that's not so simple once the business comes into play.

I primarily serve the tech audience. Many people in this audience are only interested in the tech stuff. They don't want any non-tech stuff. From a business standpoint, it's not wise to "spam" these tech folks with "life" articles which they "didn't sign up" for. Doing so hurts trust and relationships.

I take the relationship with you seriously — I value your time and I don't want to waste it. I also fear that sending non-tech content would alienate the very audience I worked so hard to build. So I restrained myself. Eventually (without knowing when), I suppressed so much that I don't even enjoy writing anymore.

### Why don't I create a separate blog?

I thought about it! I even bought the `zellwk.life` domain to write non-tech stuff. I never worked on this "new blog" though, because I realised a separate blog doesn't solve my problems.

Why?

Because I care about you as a fellow human being. I want to share some non-tech stuff with you — especially the ones where I noted certain lessons about life that may be helpful for you — an example is "[Why trying your best is a bad strategy](https://zellwk.com/blog/trying-your-best/)".

I hope these articles change your perspective and give you a boost. I hope they give you strength in times of need.

I also want to connect with you as a fellow human being. I want to know more about your life and share more about my life.

A separate blog doesn't solve my problem since I still want to send you these articles.

The only solution is to segment the audience based on whether they wanted non-tech articles. The only question that remains is: Should I release these articles for free?

## Considering between free vs paid non-tech articles

### Why Free

The pros of sending these articles freely are straightforward:

1. More people have a chance to read them
2. More people may resonate with these articles
3. If they resonate, the articles may help them and they would have done their job
4. Added side benefit: If people resonate with them, they may promote them

### Why not free

Four reasons:

1. People don't value free things as much as paid things. If people pay for things, they're more likely to use the advice mentioned — this is a minor point for me because... who am I to judge whether you use the content?
2. I create confusion in my offerings — why is Zell sending non-tech stuff when people what to read tech stuff? It's a dilutes the focus of the main business.
3. I have an internal struggle between producing tech content vs non-tech content — priority always goes to tech content because tech content has the potential to "bring in the money", so I never really give myself enough time to write non-tech stuff, even if they may be useful for you. Plus, I don't know whether it's useful or it's a nuisance.
4. There's no safe space — negativity hurts a lot when you're being open and vulnerable. Even though I can manage negativity, I don't like it. I don't want to waste my energy handling negativity from strangers.
5. Since there's no safe space, I cannot be completely honest with myself — this reduces the quality of the non-tech stuff. We've seen too many fluffy feel-good motivational stuff out there. Where's the reality in all that? I wish to be more real, and I can only do this within a safe space.

### Why not paid

The reasons are pretty obvious here:

1. Putting stuff behind a paywall means less people will read them.
2. I may be labelled as a money grubber kind-of-person — this is already happening from the responses I've seen. I believe this is a minor thing since it's mostly miscommunication, which I'm addressing in this email anyway.
3. The whole membership thing may flop — maybe nobody wants values the non-tech stuff I write. If the membership project fails this way, it's a good thing since I can shelf this project away once and for all for this audience.

### Why Paid

Lots of great reasons

1. People value things they pay for, so there's a higher chance of them using it.
2. It reduces confusion between my offerings — I can separate the content cleanly while occasionally releasing sneak peeks of paid content. When people have to pay, they will recognize the separation between content types.
3. I can create a safe space where I'm able to dive deeper, be more honest, and be more vulnerable about things — when I'm honest and vulnerable, I get more clarity about my life. This is important for me.
4. When I'm honest and vulnerable, I give space for others to be honest and vulnerable too — this creates an upward ratchet of consciousness and possibility.
5. If people pay me to explore my life, it creates a positive environment for me that furthers my exploration into my life (something I'm already doing for myself, just not talking about it).

## Details of the membership and types of content

**I'm envisioning the paid membership to begin with the following:**

- At least 1 article a month
- 1 video call per quarter

I'm promising one article per month because I'm burned out and don't have lots of energy to write. I may end up producing more, but I don't want to overpromise and underdeliver at this point.

**For content, I want to focus on things that are important to me — holistic growth.**There are loads of things I can write about, I don't even know where to begin! Here are some possible topics:

- How I'm handling my depression
- Things I'm exploring and learning, along with my notes or recommendations
- What I'm inspired by
- Things I'm pondering about with my life and questions I'm asking myself
- Lessons I learned about stuff that happened recently
- Behind the scenes of my work / business / life
- Updates on projects I'm working on

There are three ways to go about choosing topics:

- I write whatever I want
- You ask questions and I answer them
- We'll do both (or alternate between them)

### The video calls

**The video calls each quarter help us connect with each other.**

Writing has became a lonely job for me — I yearn to connect more of you, but I simply can't jump onto random 1-1 calls with strangers anymore. I tried that during the start of the pandemic. It doesn't work for me because I met many vampires who sucked my energy dry within a single call.

I'm keeping it to one call each quarter to make sure things are sustainable on my end.

We can talk about anything on this video call. Examples include:

- Talking about this membership — how to make this more useful for you
- Talking about general issues in life
- Going in depth into some of my articles

### Connecting with other members

Some people expressed interest in connecting with other members in the survey.

**Ultimately, I want this membership to become a platform where we can connect as conscious individuals.** We can promote each other's growth, exchange ideas, and support each other.

This goal is still far away. We don't even know whether this platform is going to take off successfully at this point, so I don't want to get ahead of myself.

We can check if there's interest between members connecting with each other during the quarterly calls. If there is great interest, we'll work to find a way to create a place where we can connect better.

## So what are you paying for?

There are a myriad of things you are paying for.

- You're paying for access into my experiences through my writing or videos
- You're paying to get answers to the questions you have
- You're paying to build a community platform if you wish to become part of it.
- You're buying into an environment that supports authenticity, integrity, and holistic growth — both for me and you.

Is it worth it to invest in this membership?

If you're asking this question after reading everything I wrote above, the answer for you is most probably no. Keep your money and do something else with it.

### I want to join, but I can't afford $10/month

Many people expressed concerned about the pricing at $10/month. I hear you. Currency exchange rates can be a huge problem for some people.

There are two solutions to handle this problem:

- Implementing Parity Purchasing Power (PPP) which adjusts the membership fee according to the country you're in.
- Creating several payment tiers. Here's a possible tier structure
  - Tier 1: $3 — Gain access to read content
  - Tier 2: $5 — Gets ability to ask questions which I would seriously answer when I'm able to. Unfortunately, I can't promise I'll answer everything. You also get access to quarterly call recordings
  - Tier 3: $10 — Gain access to attend quarterly calls in person (except for the first call, which would be open to everyone)

Which would you prefer? I'd love to hear your thoughts on them.

## Quick Summary

**This membership aims to create an environment that promotes introspection and holistic growth**. It gives me the chance to look deeper into myself, understand myself more, and expressed myself in ways I couldn't have expressed before.

**The current membership structure looks like this:**

- 1 article per month (more if I can manage it)
- 1 video calls every quarter

We have no idea how this membership / community would turn out. If there's enough interest, we can create a vibrant community where we come together and create a positive ratchet for everyone.

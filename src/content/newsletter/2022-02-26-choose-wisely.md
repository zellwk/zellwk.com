---
title: Choose wisely
---

We have unlimited choices, but we have limited time, so we got to prioritize.

But there are two big problem: [We don't know what's truly important](https://ckarchive.com/b/e5uph7hzvxn9). We also prioritize based on our limitations.

Imagine the conundrum where your wife and mum fell into the water at the same time, but you can only save one of them. Who would you save?

We laugh at this silly conundrum because we know the answer — figure out a way to save both of them. (Plus, they're not idiots so they can save themselves).

But we use the same limited response when we prioritize our lives. We choose from what seems more important from a list of everything that is important — work, health, love, learning, money, family, fulfilment, etc. You name it.

If we only have time for work and family, we ignore all other important things. We believe they are out of reach. Why? Because we've been taught to do less if we cannot handle it — if we cannot juggle 2, don't think about doing 3, don't even think about doing everything.

But this reductionist approach doesn't work holistically. There are three problems with it.

First. You assume you're incapable of handling more domains. You fault yourself instead of looking for systems and solutions to become more effective.

Second. You assume you have accurate and perfect knowledge of the domains (of which you're struggling with!). But the truth is your knowledge is lacking and that's one reasons why you're struggling with it. Maybe your execution is lacking too.

Third. **You will be unhappy since you chose to ignore important things**. This unhappiness propagates and drains you. You find a way to make yourself happier (binge-eating, Netflix, etc.) instead of pursuing things that truly make you happy (which are the important things).

**You will only be truly happy if you pursue everything important to you the same time** — not literally "at the same time". What's important is you're focusing on everything important and you know you're not neglecting anything.

If we're tackling everything at once, **how can we design our lives so it become possible**? This changes your stance from "I can't do it" to "how can I do it". This changes the direction and scale of your questions, and the answers that come (or even questions that come) will point you in the direction of a wonderful life.

**It's possible to have a wonderful life.**

There's work to do.

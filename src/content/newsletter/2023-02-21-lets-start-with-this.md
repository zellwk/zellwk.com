## The Principle of Participation

Yesterday I asked you 8 questions, but many of you didn’t respond back to me with your answers.

Now some of the questions may have been sensitive and you’re unwilling to reveal them to someone you never met.

That’s perfectly fine.

But the thing is, I also told you that you can leave out your answers to the questions you weren’t comfortable with answering, so you could still have responded to the rest of the questions.

Then the question is: if you didn’t respond to me, why didn’t you respond?

- Maybe there are too many questions and you weren’t sure whether it’s worthwhile to answer everything.
- Maybe you are busy and you don’t have enough time.
- Maybe you don’t want to give up your private information to someone when you’re not even sure if he or she can help you.
- Maybe you thought I wouldn’t read what you wrote.
- Maybe you’re afraid of something…
- Or maybe it was something else…

Whatever your reason is, the truth is you’re calculating internally whether it’s worthwhile for you to respond.

What if you responded and got nothing back?

If that happens, you’ll be down with a net deficit in energy.

Nobody likes to be on the losing end, so not responding becomes the norm.

But because nobody likes to be on the losing end, if you are willing to be on the losing end — at least initially — you get to stand out from everyone else and be noticed.

When you stand out, you have a chance to get heard, be remembered, and be known — and you will only have opportunities come to you when you stand out.

Now, we can talk about standing out all day since it’s a fascinating topic and everyone wants to have answers to…

But it wouldn’t do you any good now because you won’t be able to stand out unless you’re willing to internalize and use the principle of participation first…

So let’s focus on the principle of participation for now. I promise you’ll learn how to stand out as you follow along in this email series.

Now let’s come back to participation.

## What is Participation?

Participation happens when you invest your energy to take part in something — for example, interacting with something or having a conversation with someone.

In this particular case, it’s about whether you are investing your energy in our conversation here — to sit down, answer the questions, and respond back to me.

When you participate, you will notice that the more energy you invest in the activity you’re taking part in, the more energy will be returned back to you.

This will show up in many places:

- The more energy you put into learning, the more you’ll learn.
- The more energy you put into a conversation, the deeper and more meaningful the conversation will be.

You don’t have to go far to see this principle working.

Just remember the last time you had a meaningful discussion with someone that you thoroughly enjoyed.

- When was it?
- What was it about?
- Why did you find it so enjoyable?

Well, the real reason you found it enjoyable was because both of you participated in the conversation — so in that moment you’re giving your attention to this conversation and nothing else.

When both of you can do this, the energy from both of you begins to coalesce and it sparks joy and life.

This is one of the keys — as you will learn in the later lessons — that will allow people to enjoy working with you.

That’s because communication is the basis of our species’ evolution.

- It is how we humans won over all species of life and dominated the planet.
- It is also how we have come together to come up with all these amazing inventions we have in this world.

If you chose not to participate, then you would have discarded one of the most important things that have allowed humans to flourish — and as you will soon see, will allow you to flourish.

And that’s your first lesson on participation.

You need to stand out from the crowd to be noticed. You need to be noticed to be picked for the job.

Unfortunately, everyone tries to stand out in the same way — you try to become better developers. You work on your technical skills. You try to write a good resume. You build a good portfolio, etc.

These activities build your case against other people. But other people are also doing the same thing against you. So it’s normal to conclude that you have to become the best of the best in order to be picked for a job.

But everyone tries to become the best of the best.

Everyone tries to do the same thing.

**Since you do the same thing as everyone else, you don’t stand out. You actually blend in.**

When you blend in, you cannot stand out.

Even if you are the best technical engineer in a given pool of applications, you are nothing but a sheet of paper in the eyes of a recruiter. They glance through a whole stack of resumes and you’re simply one of them. If they don’t spot anything spectacular within seconds, you’re out.

It’s depressing because there is only one job and you’re competing against hundreds of other applicants. That’s why the chances of getting picked are so low. That’s why people say you should apply for more jobs in hopes of getting picked.

Hope is not a very good strategy ya?

So people begin to talk about building up a body of work — a portfolio, so to speak. But when you’re starting out, everyone has a similar portfolio. You build a couple of small projects and that’s about it. So you can’t truly stand out with a portfolio. You blend in instead.

To truly stand out with a portfolio, you need to have a large body of work behind you. For example, I stand out pretty strongly against other people because I have a good blog. You probably came to me because you got recommended to one of my articles. That’s how you stand out with a body of work.

At this point it gets even demoralizing because it seems like you have to become great — maybe a fraction of who I am, or a fraction of the heroes you’re looking up to — in order to stand out and get picked for a job.

But that doesn’t make you stand out either. Why? Because many people figured that out as well — so again, you’re competing in a sea of sameness where everyone tries to outdo each other by writing blogs and creating articles and Youtube videos and basically trying to create some sort of portfolio to back them up.

**The truth is: You don’t stand out because you don’t understand the game that is being played around you.**

There’s something far more effective than what’s obvious.

What’s obvious is building your technical skills, building up your resume, building up your body of work.

These are important. I’m not saying they’re not important. But there is a critical element that you’re missing which is why you don’t see how you can stand out.

**The art of getting noticed relies on factors that are not about blending in — but about truly standing out.**

Have you noticed how I stand out in this email by not blending in and talking about building good resumes, CVs, networking, and a portfolio of work?

That’s how you stand out.

Now I’ve given you a framework of how to think about standing out.

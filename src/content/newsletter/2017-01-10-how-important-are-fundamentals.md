---
title: How important are fundamentals?
---

“Fundamentals are important.”

You’ve probably heard this a thousand times, so much that you can recite them in your sleep. Zzz…

I thought I knew the importance of fundamentals too! But I was wrong. I didn’t realize how important they were.

When I started learning to code, I tried to create websites as quickly as I can, but I found myself frustrated by my lack of skills. I quit. I thought I’d never be able to learn to code. I’m just not smart enough.

A few years later, I tried to pick up coding again. This time, everything was different. I was able to learn quickly even though I still encountered a lot of difficulties. In three years, I learned enough to write books and give talks at conferences.

What changed?

**A focus on fundamentals.**

(Ok, I didn’t know I was focusing on fundamentals. But thinking back, that was the crucial change).

Fundamentals helped by giving me a framework to think with. This framework helped me **learn faster**, **remember better**, **solve problems quicker** and **ask better questions**.

How?

Well, to learn web fundamentals means to learn the basic principles the web operates with. It’s similar to learning laws of nature that are present in our world.

For instance, I’m sure you’ve heard of Newton’s three laws of motion. Without these three laws, you won’t understand why apples drop from trees. It just wouldn’t make sense. The web also contains these “laws” which you can use as a guiding framework.

Fundamentals help you _learn quickly_ by helping you understand what appears to be memory work. Using our Newton’s laws of motion example, we learned and understood why the Earth revolves around the Sun. We can also calculate how long it takes. Without these three laws, we’ll probably still be clueless.

Next, because you understood what you learned, you have a point of reference which help you access that knowledge quickly. This is how fundamentals help with _memory_. Now you know why there are times where you learn something, but forget it almost immediately.

Third, you _solve problems quicker_ when you know your fundamentals because you’re likely able to remember the solution. Failing that, you’re still likely to be able to work out the answer without consulting Google. In the worst case scenario where you can’t do either, you’ll still have an easier time searching for answers since you know what to watch out for.

Finally, with good fundamentals, you can [ask better questions](https://zellwk.com/blog/asking-questions/) since you know what information to provide to the person who’s answering them.

So, in my opinion, fundamentals are essential. Once you master your fundamentals, you’ll learn faster, remember what you’ve learned, solve problems quicker and ask better questions.

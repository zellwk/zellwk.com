Yesterday you learned an important concept that will change the way you go about getting jobs forever.

The concept goes like this:

1. Nobody wants to hire anyone.
2. They will only hire someone when they need them.

**When you understand this, you’ll begin to understand what employers and clients are actually looking for at the deepest levels**.

And when you’re able to see what employers and clients truly want, you’ll automatically why most of the “required” things — experience, certificates, portfolios, resumes, interviews, etc. — are simply proxies for employers and clients to guess whether you can give them what they want.

**When you see this, you’ll understand that you’ll be able to clinch the job(s) regardless of most of the factors you’ve been worried about** — years of experience, age, certificates, etc — as long as you’re able to provide them with what they truly want.

And here’s the thing — **deep down inside you already know what it is**, but you’re unable to express or see it because everyone else tells you differently.

Today, we’re going to peel off these layers for you and you’ll become someone who’s able to give employers and clients what they need, and therefore, get the job(s) that you want.

Let’s go.

## We’re going to begin by expanding the concept you already know

So far, we have established the following axioms

1. Nobody wants to hire anyone.
2. They will only hire someone when they need them.

So, if a company puts out a job advertisement, It must mean they need someone.

And **if they need someone, they’re actually desperate for help deep down inside** — especially if they’re looking for help through a job advertisement.

It’s easy to see why.

That’s because if you need water, but cannot find water, you’re going to be desperate for water. If you need to, you’ll go out of your way to search for water.

This is true for employers and clients as well

When they need help, they’ll want to find help around them. If they cannot help around them, they’ll post on job boards as a last resort.

**This is how we know that employers and clients are actually quite desperate when they post on job boards** — they simply have no other ways to find quality people for help.

Now, if job boards are a last resort, it must also mean that there are other avenues that employers and clients prefer — and if you’re able to get their attention before they have to post on job boards, the job will be yours.

This leads to a lesson on how to best find employers and clients… and we’ll talk about this in a later email because what we’re covering today is even more important.

## Here’s What Employers and Clients Actually Need Help With

There are only two reasons why you would pay anyone to do something for you:

1. You don’t like to do it
2. You can’t do it

If you don’t like to do something, and if you have enough resources to spare, you’ll spend money to hire someone to do the work for you — so you can spend the time elsewhere to do something of a higher value.

For example, many people hire maids to clean their houses and wash their clothes so they can spend their time learning and making money.

Next, if you cannot do something, you have the option to learn it. But if learning takes too much time, and you can do something of a higher value with that time, you’ll choose to hire someone instead of learning that thing.

For example, most employers and clients don’t want to invest their time into learning code. They want to spend their time working on their businesses or learning how to improve their businesses instead.

**This is why employers and clients hire you** — because they want to buy back some of their time.

And this is also why many people buy books and courses — because it helps them cut short the amount of time spent learning and therefore increases the amount of time spent in money-producing activities.

**This is what people usually call an investment mindset** — it’s not investing in the stock market or anything. It’s really about using money to buy time, so you can invest that time in something more worthwhile.

Now it may seem like we’ve digressed a bit, but we haven’t. That’s because this is an important concept that ties into what you need to know next.

## Nobody likes to hire through job boards because it’s a lot of work

Employers and clients have to spend a lot of time and energy when they hire through job boards:

- They have to write job advertisements and put them up on the job boards.
- They have to sieve through hundreds and thousands of applications to find the one.
- They have to make snap decisions and risk failing to find the one because of these decisions.
- They have to conduct interviews with potential employees that can last 30 mins to 1 hour for each person.
- If there’s more than one round of interviews, that’s another 30 mins to 1 hour extra spent in that person in the hiring process…

And this is just the amount of work they have to do before they even hire anyone!

There’s more. After hiring,

- They have to train the hired person
- They have to pay the person for their work
- They have to judge whether they’ve hired the right person or not.
- If they hired the wrong person, then all the time and energy spent hiring this person becomes wasted…
- And they have to fire the person and start over…

And, it gets worse.

Even if they hired the right person for the job, when the person leaves after being well-trained, the employer or client will have to redo the entire hiring process…

So **a lot of time, energy, and money are invested into finding people for work** — which means that hiring is an extremely stressful process for employers and clients.

But even though hiring is extremely stressful, employers and clients are still able to adopt the investment mindset, bite the bullet, and do it anyway — because they see the benefit the investment.

That’s because **time is extremely important** since it’s a finite resource for all of us — we only have 24 hours of that every day.

With this understanding, you can begin to see what employers actually want.

**They want you to give them time** — much more time than they have to give up to invest in you.

This is why employers and clients often inflate job requirements to insane levels — they simply hope to find unicorns that will give them time.

Now, knowing that employers and clients are actually looking for time will give you huge benefits when positioning yourself in front of other candidates — that’s because you’re extremely attractive to them compared to everyone else who’s peddling lemons.

## Baseline Qualities You Need To Have

You just learned that the hiring process is an extremely stressful activity — where employers and clients have to invest huge amounts of time, energy, and money.

This means they have incurred an immense energy deficit when trying to hire someone.

With such high stakes, the thought at the back of their minds won’t just be “can this person do the job”.

No.

**The actual thought at the back of the employer’s or client’s mind is: “Can I depend on you?”**

Nothing else matters if they cannot depend on you.

Now the question is a bit too broad, so what we can do is split the question into three major categories based on what we just said earlier:

- **Can I depend on you to do the job?**
- **Can I depend on you to not leave me?**
- **Can I depend on you to work together well with me and the rest of my team?**

When they’re testing whether someone can do the job, they’ll check for things such as:

- How much you’re able to understand the requirements of the job
- How much you’re able to deliver the job
- Whether the delivery is accurate to the requirements
- How thorough you are when you work
- What have you done in the past to prove you’re capable of the job
- How responsive you are to them
- How much willingness you show in helping them

When they’re testing if someone will stay with them, they’ll check for things such as:

- Whether your dreams align with theirs
- Whether you have the intention to leave the company
- Whether you wish to work long term
- Whether you have a track record completing work when times get tough
- How many companies did you leave before
- How much time did you spend in a company before leaving
- Why are you trying to leave your current company now

And when they’re testing whether someone will work well with them and their team, they’ll check for things such as:

- Your personality
- Whether you shirk away from responsibilities. accept them, or thrive in them
- What’s your level of initiative
- How do you behave when around peers and superiors
- How do you behave when you are alone
- Whether you drain them of energy or give them energy
- Whether you’re a nice person to be with
- Whether you’ll conflict with each other or support each other…

What we have here isn’t an exhaustive list. There are more factors to list, but you already get the drift.

Now, **they will only give you a chance if they judge that you’re able to meet their requirements** in these three necessary categories.

If you pass these requirements, then you compete on the next level with the rest to see whether you’re able to give the most time and energy surplus to the employer and client.

And that’s a higher level that we’ll discuss another time because you need to be able to fulfill these basic criteria first.

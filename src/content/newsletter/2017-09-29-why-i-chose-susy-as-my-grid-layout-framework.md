---
title: Why I chose Susy as my grid layout framework
---

It’s tough to choose a grid system to work with, but you still gotta choose one at the end of the day. It’s up to you what you choose.

Before you decide though, I thought it’ll be helpful for you if I ran through how I made my decision to use Susy.

Before the switch, I was already using Susyone. Here are some of my thoughts:

I mainly work on the frontend and I code up alot of custom designs for my clients. Sometimes these designs can get super complicated, hence I wanted a grid system that can be powerful enough create these complex designs quickly. Ideally, this grid system should work in every project I did, no matter what was thrown at me.

In short, I was looking for flexibility.

Susyone was very similar to Neat. They pretty much worked the same way and had the same features. So there wasn’t a need for me to change to Neat at all.

My choices then boiled down to Singularity and Susy.

Both Singularity and Susy were awesome and allowed me to customize my grids in any way I wanted, including building asymmetric grids.

Susy however, was more feature rich. It has everything Singularity provides, plus everything you can do with Susyone (or Neat).

To top it off, the creators of Singularity contributed to Susy, along with multiple other smart people, which prompted me to go for Susy 2.

Since then, I’ve learned so much about using Susy that I can use it to create any grid I can think of.

There wasn’t a need to look for another grid system anymore, and that’s why I decided to write the [Learning Susy book](http://zell-weekeat.com/learnsusy/), to help you get to the same level where I’m at right now.

## What if you’re still not sure?

It’s normal to be unsure, it’s scary to learn something new.

I didn’t know whether Susy will be right for me before I tried it. It was a big leap of faith. There was no turning back once I mastered it though.

You probably wont know for sure whether you want to use Susy until you’ve tried it out.

If you don’t give Susy a try, you’ll probably keep wondering whether you should switch to Susy as you continue digging for another framework. This takes up a lot of brain power and energy that you can use for more important things.

If you do decide to try Susy, I’ll recommend that you [pick up a copy of the Learning Susy book](http://zell-weekeat.com/learnsusy/) and go through it. You’ll get Susy to work with you way faster than painfully trying to understand everything from scratch.

And well, what’s the worse that could happen if you decide to give Susy a try? :)

Now, it’s your turn to make a choice.

I hope you choose to use Susy.
